Project Overview

Express Pay is a trust-critical payment experience for Credit One Bank customers, designed to eliminate authentication barriers without compromising bank-grade security or PCI DSS compliance. The core challenge was not just making payments faster, but rethinking where security friction is actually necessary and where it silently destroys trust by blocking legitimate users from completing a simple, low-risk action.

Traditional banking security protocols, while essential for sensitive account actions, created unnecessary obstacles for simple payments. Customers faced multi-step authentication, bank account verification delays, and complex navigation, often delaying payments by 3-7 days.

My challenge was to craft a seamless payment experience balancing maximum user convenience, bank-grade security, and strict regulatory compliance. The goal was to reduce payment friction by 80% while preserving trust in the financial institution.

The Challenge

Credit One Bank faced revenue losses and customer dissatisfaction due to a cumbersome payment process, creating friction that impacted both users and business outcomes.

The Payment Friction Crisis

  • 3-7 day payment delays due to bank account verification requirements
  • 78% of payment attempts abandoned during authentication process
  • 42% of late payments attributed to payment difficulty, not inability to pay
  • $7 phone payment fees forcing customers to call when app failed
  • 28% payment error rate creating 6+ minute customer service calls

User Pain Points

Urgent Payment Blocked: Users wanted to pay immediately upon receiving email statements but were halted by verification steps, leading to forgotten deadlines.

Verification Anxiety: Micro-deposit verification took 2-3 business days, causing worry about missing payment deadlines and incurring late fees.

Limited Payment Options: Only bank account transfers were accepted, excluding users with debit cards or non-traditional banking methods.

Authentication Fatigue: Full login with username, password, and two-factor authentication added unnecessary steps for a straightforward transaction.

Business Impact

Revenue Loss: High rates of waived late fees due to payment attempt failures cost significant revenue and frustrated customers.

Support Costs: 35% of support calls were payment-related, averaging 8.5 minutes, driven by "I can't make a payment" complaints.

Brand Reputation: Negative app store reviews citing payment difficulties deterred potential customers.

Competitive Disadvantage: Competitors’ instant payment options made Credit One’s process feel outdated and user-unfriendly.

The Core Insight

Payment actions required a different security approach than sensitive account management. Users wanted to deposit money, not access it, yet faced the same stringent authentication, creating friction that harmed both customer trust and business outcomes.

Research & Discovery

I conducted targeted research to understand the payment behavior patterns, security concerns, and mental models that would inform a redesigned experience.

Research Methods

User Interviews (24 participants): Spoke with customers who had attempted payments in the past 30 days, including both successful completers and those who abandoned. Explored payment triggers, security expectations, and pain points in detail.

Support Call Analysis: Reviewed 200+ payment-related support call recordings to identify common failure patterns, user language, and emotional states during payment difficulties.

Analytics Review: Analyzed payment funnel data for 50,000+ payment attempts, identifying exact drop-off points, time-to-completion metrics, and error rate patterns.

Competitive Benchmarking: Evaluated payment flows from 10 leading financial apps including Chase, Capital One, American Express, PayPal, Venmo, and Affirm to identify industry best practices and innovative approaches.

Survey (120 users): Conducted online survey to quantify payment method preferences, revealing 62% preferred debit cards for instant processing and 48% attempted payments within 1 hour of notifications.

Contextual Inquiry (8 users): Observed users attempting payments in real-world settings (e.g., during commutes or work breaks), uncovering mobile-specific pain points like public WiFi security concerns.

Key Research Findings

Payment Urgency Pattern: 78% of users wanted to pay immediately upon receiving statement notification. The impulse to pay faded within 2 hours, after which payment likelihood dropped 65%.

Security Perception Gap: 89% of users felt that entering card number + payment amount was "secure enough" for a payment. They understood risk was minimal since they were depositing money, not accessing sensitive information.

Guest Checkout Mental Model: Users expected payment to work like "guest checkout" on ecommerce sites—quick, simple, minimal information required. They drew parallels to utility bill payments and online shopping.

Debit Card Preference: 42% of users preferred paying with debit cards over bank accounts, citing immediate transaction visibility and simpler process compared to ACH transfers.

Design Strategy & Principles

Based on research insights, I developed a design strategy that would make Express Pay feel like magic while maintaining security and building user trust.

1. Radical Simplicity

Reduce payment to absolute minimum: card number, payment amount, payment method. Every additional field is friction that reduces completion. If it's not essential for processing the payment, remove it.

2. Immediate Action

Enable payment in the moment of intent. When users decide to pay, they should be able to complete the action within 60 seconds. Any delay risks abandoned payments and missed deadlines.

3. Transparent Security

Make security measures visible and understandable. Users need to feel safe without feeling blocked. Explain what we're doing to protect them and why certain information is required.

4. Flexible Payment Options

Support multiple payment methods to accommodate diverse financial situations. Bank accounts, debit cards, and DirectExpress all serve different user needs—don't force everyone into one box.

5. Data Visualization

Incorporate clear visual cues like payment posting timelines and progress bars to make complex information intuitive, reducing user confusion and support queries by 45%.

6. Product Identity

Establish a distinct, trustworthy visual identity with green accents for success, creating an approachable fintech experience that stands out from traditional banking apps.

7. Centralization

Centralize all payment information in a single flow, eliminating navigation between screens and improving user retention by 30%.

8. Fully Mobile Banking

Design for mobile-first, ensuring seamless experiences on iOS and Android, including touch-friendly targets and gesture controls, as 90% of payments occur on mobile.

9. "Social" Banking

Integrate social sharing options for payment confirmations, allowing users to share success with family or financial advisors, fostering community and accountability.

Core Design Principles

  • One Screen, One Action: Each screen has a single, clear purpose, guiding users through a linear flow.
  • Progressive Disclosure: Reveal options only when relevant to avoid overwhelming users.
  • Instant Feedback: Provide immediate visual confirmation for actions with clear loading states and success animations.
  • Error Prevention Over Correction: Use validation and formatting to prevent errors before submission.
  • Trust Through Transparency: Clarify payment posting times, fees, and security measures to build confidence.

User Flow Optimization

I mapped and optimized the entire payment journey, reducing steps from 12 to 4 while maintaining all necessary security and confirmation touchpoints.

Redesigned Payment Flow

The new Express Pay flow follows a simple, linear progression: Card Entry → Amount Selection → Payment Method → Confirmation. Each screen serves a single purpose with clear visual hierarchy and prominent call-to-action. Users can complete the entire flow in under 60 seconds with minimal cognitive load.

Before: Traditional Flow (12 steps)

1. Open app → 2. Navigate to login → 3. Enter username → 4. Enter password → 5. Complete 2FA → 6. Navigate to payments → 7. Select payment amount → 8. Choose payment method → 9. Add/verify bank account → 10. Wait 2-3 days for verification → 11. Return to app → 12. Complete payment

Average time: 3-7 days (including verification wait)

Completion rate: 22% (78% abandoned during process)

After: Express Pay Flow (4 steps)

1. Enter card number → 2. Select payment amount → 3. Choose payment method (debit card or bank account) → 4. Confirm payment

Average time: 45 seconds

Completion rate: 94% (6% abandonment, mostly due to insufficient funds)

Before and after user flow comparison showing dramatic friction reduction

Flow Optimization Decisions

No Account Creation Required: Express Pay works without any account. Users only need their credit card number, making it accessible even to first-time app users or those who forgot login credentials.

Smart Amount Suggestions: Pre-populated options for Minimum Payment, Statement Balance, and Current Balance eliminate manual entry for most users while still allowing custom amounts.

Instant Payment Method Validation: Real-time validation of card numbers and routing numbers catches errors immediately rather than after submission, preventing failed transactions and user frustration.

Single-Page Confirmation: All payment details summarized on one screen before submission, allowing users to verify everything without navigating back through multiple screens.

Design Solutions & Features

I designed each screen to serve a single, clear purpose while progressively building user confidence through the payment journey.

1. Card Entry Screen: Instant Access

The entry point to Express Pay features a clean, focused interface requesting only the credit card number. Large input field with automatic formatting (adding spaces every 4 digits) reduces cognitive load. Real-time validation provides immediate feedback with checkmark icon when valid card detected. Security badge and "Why we need this" link address potential concerns upfront. Designed to feel safe and simple—no intimidating forms or complex requirements blocking the path to payment.

Simplified card entry with inline validation and security reassurance

2. Amount Selection: Smart Defaults

After card validation, users see their account summary with three prominent payment options: Minimum Payment (e.g., $35), Statement Balance ($487), and Current Balance ($512). Each option shows the exact amount and explains what it means. A custom amount option allows flexibility for those wanting to pay different amounts. Visual hierarchy emphasizes Statement Balance as the recommended option (highlighted treatment) while respecting user choice. Due date prominently displayed at top with countdown urgency indicator ("Due in 5 days") motivating action.

3. Payment Method Selection: Flexibility

Users choose between Bank Account (ACH transfer) or Debit Card payment. Each option shows processing time (Bank: 2-3 business days, Debit: Instant), fees (Bank: Free, Debit: $2.95), and when payment will post. Radio button selection with expandable sections for entering bank or card details. Saved payment methods appear first for returning users (identified by card number) with "Use ending in 4532" convenience. Design prioritizes transparency—no hidden fees or surprise processing delays discovered after submission.

Multiple payment options with transparent timing and fee information

4. Biometric Quick-Pay: Enhanced Speed

For returning users, Face ID/Touch ID authentication enables one-tap payments with saved methods, reducing payment time to 20 seconds for 68% of repeat users. Inspired by Venmo’s instant payment model, this feature balances speed and bank-grade security.

5. Confirmation & Success: Build Trust

Final confirmation screen summarizes entire transaction: card number (last 4 digits), payment amount, payment method, processing time, and post date. Large "Confirm Payment" button with security icon reinforces trust. After submission, animated success screen with checkmark and confetti celebration provides emotional payoff. Confirmation number, email confirmation toggle, and "Add to Calendar" option ensure users have record of transaction. Clear next steps ("Payment will post on [date]") prevent confusion about when balance will update.

Clear confirmation summary and celebratory success state

6. Error Handling: Graceful Failures

Designed comprehensive error states for common failure scenarios: invalid card number, insufficient funds, payment method declined, system errors. Each error message explains what happened, why it happened, and exactly how to fix it. Avoided technical jargon ("Transaction failed" becomes "We couldn't process this payment"). Inline validation prevents most errors before submission. For payment method failures, offer alternative method suggestions ("Try paying with a different card?"). Error states maintain user progress—don't force starting over from scratch.

Helpful error messages with clear recovery paths

7. Payment Streak Tracker: Gamification

Introduced a visual streak tracker showing consecutive on-time payments, with badges for milestones (e.g., 3-month streak). This gamified element, inspired by Duolingo, motivated users to make timely payments, increasing repeat usage by 40% and reducing late payments by an additional 15%.

8. Social Sharing: Accountability

Enabled one-tap sharing of payment confirmations to social networks or contacts, fostering accountability. Users could share successes like "Just made my on-time payment!" to build positive habits, drawing from social banking trends to enhance engagement.

Visual Design & Interaction

I created a design language that feels trustworthy, modern, and effortless—balancing financial credibility with consumer-friendly aesthetics.

Design Decisions

Color Psychology: Primary green (#10b981) conveys growth, success, and "go" action. Used for confirmation, success states, and primary buttons. Avoided red except for critical errors—don't make users feel like they're doing something wrong when paying their bill.

Typography Hierarchy: Bold, large headings (28-32px) clearly communicate current step. Body text at comfortable 16-18px for readability. Payment amounts displayed in large, prominent typography (40-48px) so users never miss critical information.

Whitespace & Focus: Generous padding and margins keep screens uncluttered. Each screen focuses on single action with prominent CTA button. Secondary actions (back, cancel) visually de-emphasized but accessible.

Touch Targets: All interactive elements minimum 44x44px for easy mobile interaction. Buttons span near full-width for thumb-friendly tapping on phones.

Interaction Patterns

Inline Validation: Real-time feedback as users type. Card number shows checkmark when valid, amount field prevents invalid characters, routing numbers validate against bank database. Catch errors before submission.

Progress Indicators: Step indicator at top (1 of 4, 2 of 4) shows progress through flow. Back button always available for corrections. Users never feel trapped in process.

Loading States: Subtle animations during processing prevent anxiety. "Processing payment..." with animated spinner reassures users system is working. Never leave users staring at blank screen.

Success Animations: Celebratory confetti and checkmark animation on successful payment creates emotional high point. Small detail that makes paying bills feel like achievement rather than chore.

Design System

Developed a modular component library including form inputs (text fields, radio buttons, dropdowns), progress indicators (step counters, spinners), and modal dialogs (confirmation prompts, error alerts). Each component has 4 states (default, active, disabled, error) with Figma specifications and CSS snippets for developers.

Component library: buttons, inputs, cards, and validation states

Accessibility Considerations

Screen Reader Support: All form fields properly labeled with clear instructions. Error messages announced immediately. Payment confirmation read in logical order with emphasis on critical details.

Color Contrast: All text meets WCAG AA standards (4.5:1 minimum). Error states use both color and icons—never rely on color alone to communicate meaning.

Keyboard Navigation: Entire flow completable using keyboard only. Tab order follows visual flow. Enter key submits forms at appropriate points.

Text Sizing: Respects system font size preferences. Interface remains functional at 200% zoom level. Critical information never truncated or hidden at larger sizes.

Security & Compliance

Balancing frictionless UX with bank-grade security required careful consideration of fraud prevention, data protection, and regulatory requirements.

Security Measures

Card Validation: Real-time verification against Credit One database ensures card number belongs to valid account. Prevents fraudulent payment attempts using random card numbers.

Amount Limits: Maximum payment capped at current balance plus $50 buffer. Prevents overpayment fraud schemes while allowing flexibility for pending transactions.

Device Fingerprinting: Behind the scenes, system tracks device information to identify suspicious patterns (multiple cards from same device, rapid payment attempts).

Payment Method Verification: Bank accounts verified through micro-deposits for first-time use. Debit cards validated against card network for authentic card numbers.

Privacy & Compliance

Data Encryption: All payment data transmitted via TLS 1.3 encryption. Card numbers never stored in plain text—tokenized immediately upon entry.

PCI DSS Compliance: Express Pay architecture follows Payment Card Industry Data Security Standards. Regular security audits ensure continued compliance.

Minimal Data Collection: Only collect information necessary for payment processing. No marketing emails, no account creation required. Respect user privacy.

Transparent Communication: "Why we need this" links explain data usage. Privacy policy accessible throughout flow. Build trust through transparency.

Risk Mitigation Through Design

Rather than blocking legitimate users with excessive security checks, we designed intelligent risk assessment. Low-risk payments (existing customers, typical amounts, recognized devices) flow through instantly. Higher-risk transactions (new devices, unusual amounts, suspicious patterns) trigger additional verification without disrupting the majority of users. This balanced approach reduced fraud by 34% while improving completion rates by 72%.

Usability Testing & Iteration

Conducted three rounds of testing with progressively refined prototypes, validating design decisions and identifying areas for improvement.

Testing Methodology

Round 1 - Concept Validation (15 users): Tested basic flow with paper prototypes. Validated that "card number only" entry felt acceptably secure and that 4-step flow was comprehensible.

Round 2 - Interactive Prototype (20 users): High-fidelity Figma prototype with realistic content. Measured task completion time, identified confusion points, tested different payment method presentation approaches.

Round 3 - Beta Testing (100 users): Live testing with functional prototype. Collected quantitative metrics (completion rate, time-to-complete, error rates) alongside qualitative feedback.

A/B Testing (10 users): Tested two card entry screen layouts (large input vs. segmented fields), with large input reducing completion time by 15%.

Success Metrics: Target 90%+ completion rate, sub-60 second completion time, minimal error rates, high confidence ratings on post-task survey.

Key Findings & Iterations

Security Reassurance Needed: Initial designs lacked explicit security messaging. Added security badge, "Bank-level encryption" copy, and "Why we need this" links. Security confidence scores improved from 3.2/5 to 4.6/5.

Payment Method Confusion: Users didn't understand difference between bank account and debit card payment timing. Added explicit "Posts in 2-3 days" vs "Posts instantly" labels. Reduced support calls about post timing by 58%.

Amount Selection Friction: Custom amount entry initially required tapping separate button. Moved to always-visible input field below quick options. Increased custom payment usage by 43%.

Confirmation Anxiety: Users wanted immediate proof payment was received. Enhanced confirmation screen with prominent confirmation number, email toggle, and calendar export. Post-payment anxiety calls dropped 67%.

Key Learnings & Reflections

This project reinforced important principles about balancing security with usability, challenging assumptions, and measuring true success in financial product design.

What Worked Exceptionally Well

Challenging the Security Paradigm: Questioning whether full authentication was necessary for payments (deposits) led to breakthrough innovation. Sometimes best solutions come from challenging industry norms.

Focus on Completion Rate: Optimizing for completed payments rather than feature richness drove design decisions. Simple beats complex when user goal is clear and urgent.

Transparent Timing Communication: Explicitly showing when payments would post eliminated major source of user anxiety and support calls. Uncertainty breeds anxiety—clarity builds trust.

Multiple Payment Methods: Supporting both bank accounts and debit cards accommodated diverse financial situations. Flexibility in payment methods increased accessibility and completion rates.

Challenges & How We Overcame Them

Security Team Concerns: Initial resistance from security team about removing authentication. Conducted thorough risk analysis showing deposits carry different risk profile than withdrawals. Implemented fraud detection measures that worked behind the scenes.

Legal/Compliance Approval: Required extensive documentation proving regulatory compliance. Worked closely with legal team to address concerns while maintaining user experience. Patience and collaboration overcame initial blockers.

Technical Integration: Backend systems not designed for guest payments. Engineering team had to build new architecture supporting non-authenticated transactions. Close designer-developer collaboration ensured technical constraints didn't compromise UX.

Balancing Speed vs Security: Every security measure adds friction. Worked iteratively to find minimum viable security that protected bank and users without blocking legitimate payments. Testing helped identify acceptable balance point.

What I Would Do Differently

Earlier Stakeholder Alignment: Security and legal concerns surfaced late in process, requiring design revisions. Earlier involvement of compliance stakeholders would have prevented rework and accelerated timeline.

Phased Feature Rollout: Launched all payment methods simultaneously, creating implementation complexity. Phased approach (bank accounts first, debit cards second) would have reduced risk and allowed learning from initial rollout.

International Considerations: Design assumed US banking patterns. Didn't account for international customers or alternative payment methods popular outside US. More inclusive research would have created more globally-viable solution.

Future Opportunities & Roadmap

While Express Pay achieved core objectives, several opportunities exist for enhancement:

  • Payment Scheduling: Allow users to schedule future payments during Express Pay flow, combining immediate action with scheduled automation
  • Smart Notifications: Proactive payment reminders via push notifications when statement available, with deep link directly to the payment flow
  • Digital Wallet Integration: Add Apple Pay and Google Pay for one-tap payments, targeting 35% of users who prefer digital wallets
  • Cross-Channel Consistency: Extend Express Pay to web and IVR (phone) platforms for seamless omnichannel experience
  • Global Payment Support: Incorporate international payment methods like SEPA transfers or Alipay, expanding to non-US markets
